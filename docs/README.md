# OctoAcme Project Management Docs

A central entry point for the OctoAcme project management processes and guidance used across the project lifecycle.

## Project management process summary

OctoAcme uses a lightweight, iterative delivery model that starts with project initiation. Teams validate the business need, define measurable outcomes, align stakeholders, and document the early plan in a project one-pager. At this stage, the group clarifies the problem, success metrics, key dependencies, and resource needs so there is a clear basis for whether the work should move forward.

Once approved, work moves into planning. Teams break the initiative into prioritized backlog items with acceptance criteria, estimate scope, identify risks and dependencies, and define the Definition of Done. This planning phase turns the concept into a concrete roadmap with milestones, owner accountability, and a shared understanding of what must be delivered.

During execution, work is tracked on a project board and reviewed in regular team and stakeholder communication. The process emphasizes clear ownership, small deliverables, frequent feedback, and rapid adaptation. Developers, QA, and project leads collaborate to build, test, and review work, while PMs and product leads keep progress aligned with the plan and escalate blockers when needed.

Release and deployment follow readiness checks, validation, and rollback planning to reduce operational risk. After shipping, the team captures lessons learned through retrospectives and tracks improvement actions back into the backlog or issue tracker. This lifecycle keeps the work customer-focused, data-informed, and continuously improving.

## Core roles and working model

OctoAcme’s process relies on clear roles and shared accountability:

- Project Manager (PM): coordinates delivery, schedules, communication, and risk management
- Product Manager / Product Lead: defines outcomes, priorities, and success metrics
- Developers: design, build, test, and deliver software changes
- QA / Testing: verify acceptance criteria and quality expectations
- Stakeholders: provide input, sponsorship, and approvals

The operating model values iterative delivery, psychological safety, and transparent communication. Teams use project boards, standups, weekly updates, demos, and retrospectives to maintain alignment across execution.

## Quality assurance and communication practices

Quality is built into delivery from the beginning. Pull requests are expected to be focused, include issue links and acceptance criteria, and pass CI checks before review. Unit tests, integration tests, and smoke tests are used as appropriate to reduce the chance of defects reaching production. Security checks and manual QA further support confidence in releases.

Communication is structured and regular. OctoAcme recommends standing team updates, PM and product coordination, milestone reviews, and stakeholder reporting. Risks, blockers, and dependencies are tracked with owners, mitigations, and escalation paths so issues are surfaced early and handled consistently.
